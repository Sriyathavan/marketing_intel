We want these customer signals:

Customer signals:
1. Demographic
Age or age band, generation
Gender (only if self-reported)
Location: country, region, city, ZIP, urban/suburban/rural, climate zone
Time zone and language
Household size and composition: single, couple, kids and their ages, multi-generational
Pets: type and number (big for retail)
Income band or affluence proxy (ZIP-level estimates, average order value)
Homeowner or renter, home type
Education level, occupation category
Relationship status
2. Life stage and life events (the strongest timing triggers)
Moving or new home
Engagement, wedding, anniversary
New baby, pregnancy, kids reaching milestones (back to school, college)
Graduation, new job, retirement
Birthday (theirs and their household’s)
Empty nest, divorce, bereavement (handle with care; often best used to suppress content)
Health or fitness journey starts (also sensitive; see compliance)
3. Psychographic
Values: sustainability, ethical sourcing, local, value-for-money, premium or quality
Lifestyle: active or homebody, traveler, foodie, DIY, minimalist or collector
Interests and hobbies: declared and inferred from browsing
Personality and buying style: impulse or researcher, early adopter or wait-and-see, deal hunter or convenience-first
Motivators: status, belonging, self-improvement, saving money, saving time, novelty, nostalgia
Aspirations: who they’re trying to become (fitter, more organized, better host)
Brand relationship: loyalist, switcher, fan of your brand or its values
Tone affinity: playful, aspirational, educational, no-nonsense
Social and cultural identity: communities and fandoms they identify with, holidays they observe (self-declared)
Media and influences: creators, channels and platforms they follow
4. Transactional
RFM: recency, frequency, monetary value (the backbone of consumer segmentation)
Average order value, lifetime value, predicted LTV
Product categories bought, and how deep into each
Purchase cadence and replenishment cycle (when they’ll run out)
Price sensitivity: discount-code usage, full-price vs. sale ratio
Payment method, buy-now-pay-later use
Returns rate and reasons
Basket patterns: what gets bought together
Channel: online, in-store, app, marketplace
Gifting: buying for others, and for which occasions
Subscription status: active, paused, cancelled, skipping
5. Behavioral and engagement
Email
Opens, clicks, conversions by topic, offer type and creative style
Best send time and day per person
Engagement trend: active, cooling, dormant, reactivated
Responsiveness to frequency (fatigue threshold)
Device and email client (affects layout and dark mode)
Site and app
Products and categories browsed, search terms
Abandoned carts and abandoned browsing
Wishlist and saved items
Price-drop and back-in-stock watching
Size, color and fit choices
Time on site, return visits, depth of session
Quiz or style-profile results
App push and SMS engagement (for cross-channel coordination)
Social and community
User-generated content, reviews written, ratings given
Referrals sent and redeemed
Social follows and engagement with brand accounts
Event and pop-up attendance
6. Loyalty and relationship
Loyalty tier, points balance, points expiry dates
Lifecycle stage: subscriber only, first-time buyer, repeat, VIP, lapsed, win-back
Signup source and the offer that brought them in
Customer service history: complaints, how they were resolved, sentiment
NPS and survey responses
Churn risk score
7. Declared preferences (zero-party data, the most reliable)
Stated interests and categories
Preferred content type: deals, new arrivals, how-tos, stories
Preferred frequency and channels
Sizes, skin type, dietary needs, style preferences (dietary and health-related ones count as sensitive; see compliance)
Birthday and key dates they’ve shared
Goals (“I want to…”) captured in quizzes and onboarding
8. Contextual and external timing
Local weather and season (very strong for apparel, food, home and travel)
Local and cultural holidays
Payday cycles (often inferred from purchase timing)
Major shopping moments: Black Friday, Prime Day, back to school, Valentine’s
Local events, sports seasons
Inventory and product-launch calendar on your side
