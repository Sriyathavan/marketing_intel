Email Channel Strategy Brief: Q4 2026 – Q1 2027
Brand: Trailmark Expeditions (fictional), small-group outdoor and adventure travel Planning window: Nov 2, 2026 – Jan 30, 2027 (90 days) Owner: Lifecycle & Email Marketing Version: 1.0 Data: Synthetic, for prototype use only

Purpose: This brief gives the Marketing Intelligence Engine the overall goals, audiences, product priorities and constraints for the email channel. Every opportunity and campaign the engine generates should trace back to it.

Scope: This brief covers the email channel only. Other digital channels (paid, social, search ads, SMS, push), their performance, and how they might affect what email is asked to do are out of scope for this prototype. All targets and baselines below are email-only.

1. Business context
Trailmark runs guided small-group trips (8–14 travelers) in North America, South America and Europe. Customers book 3–9 months before they travel, and pay a deposit to reserve a spot. That means this window does two jobs:

Holiday gifting (Nov–Dec)
"Wave season" (late Dec–Jan): New Year planning, when most spring and summer 2027 trips get booked
What email is for:

Turning interest into deposits
Getting past travelers to book again
Keeping long-consideration prospects warm until they're ready
Email channel figures (2026, synthetic)	
Annual revenue from email	$10.6M
Email list size	180,000
Engaged list (opened or clicked in the last 90 days)	118,000 (66%)
Past travelers on the list	22,000
2. Channel goals (in priority order)
Revenue: hit the 90-day booking target.
Repeat bookings: get more past travelers to book a second or third trip.
Fill departures: fill the spring departures that are selling slowly before early-bird pricing ends Jan 31.
List health: keep the engaged list at 65% or higher without breaching deliverability limits.

A1: Lead with detail and reassurance: guide credentials, how hard the trip is, what's included. No pressure tactics.
A2: Lead with the experience and short dates. Urgency is fine. Keep the copy short and mobile-first.

8. Constraints and guardrails
How often people can be emailed
At most 3 emails a week per contact (2 promotional plus 1 content), including automated flows
During Black Friday week, at most 4
A5 gets at most 1 a week
Discounting
PL4 (Private & Custom): never discounted
PL1 (Guided Treks): early-bird pricing only, up to 10% off; no flash sales
PL2 (Weekend Adventures): up to 15% off, Black Friday through Cyber Monday only
PL5 (Gift Cards): a bonus of $50 back on purchases of $250 or more is allowed; no percent-off
A3 (Past Travelers) get access before discounts open, not bigger discounts
Capacity
Don't promote a departure that's 90% or more full, except to its waitlist
The Private Trips team can handle about 40 consultations a week, so PL4 sends need to be spread out
Brand voice
Grounded, expert and warm; adventurous without being reckless
No fake scarcity: urgency only when real seats or real deadlines back it up
Deliverability limits
Unsubscribe rate below 0.25% per send
Spam complaints below 0.08%
Bounce rate below 1%
Compliance
Email only contacts who opted in to marketing (CAN-SPAM, GDPR for EU contacts)
Health and fitness information (such as trip difficulty or medical notes) is never used for targeting

10. Instructions for the AI
Point every campaign to at least one audience ID and one product line ID. A campaign that doesn't is invalid.
Treat targets as goals, not predictions. Use sections 3 and 6 to rank opportunities. Don't forecast a campaign's revenue or conversion rate.
Use the scoring weights: Customer relevance 35% · Search momentum 25% · Commercial potential 25% (from section 6) · Seasonal relevance 15% (from section 7).
Keep priority and evidence confidence separate. Show both, and flag when a high-priority opportunity rests on weak evidence.
Label every statement as observed (it comes from a dataset), interpreted (an AI inference) or proposed (a recommended action).
Respect section 8. If an idea breaks a constraint, flag the conflict rather than quietly dropping or changing the idea.
Cover the full 90 days. Every week should have at least one campaign, nothing should go out on blackout dates, and each audience stays within its frequency cap.
Don't invent data. If something you need is missing, list it under "Assumptions / missing inputs."
Reflect section 2's priority order. Overall, roughly 40–45% of scheduled campaigns should target A3 or A1.
Stay within email. Don't recommend actions in other channels or reason about how they affect email; that's out of scope for this prototype.
